An interactive story about Australian television, 1959–2026

Who Really Decides What’s
Good on Australian TV?

Every year, Australian television is judged twice: once by the voting public at the TV WEEK Logie Awards, and once by the industry at the AACTA Awards. Sixty-six years of results show they rarely agree — and where they do agree, something special is happening.

Act I — The people's pick: 66 years of Gold Logies

Since 1960, TV WEEK's Gold Logie has crowned the personality Australia voted for. Which kinds of stars collect it, and has that changed?

Act II — The industry's counterpoint: the AACTAs

In 2011 the Australian Film Institute's awards became the AACTA Awards, voted by industry peers. Its television categories tell a different story about what “good” means.

Act III — The couch vs the jury room

Since 2012 both systems have run in parallel. Put their choices side by side and a pattern emerges.

Act IV — Where recognition lives

Every Gold Logie winner was born somewhere. Plotting those hometowns turns an awards history into a map of where Australia's television talent comes from.

Conclusion

Both systems are measuring different things. The couch (Logie) loves Nine's variety hosts and familiar soap faces; the jury room (AACTA) crowns ABC and streaming dramas like The Newsreader. When the two agree — on Bluey, on MasterChef, on Have You Been Paying Attention? — Australia has found something genuinely universally loved. That agreement is rare as only 11 programs have managed it.
